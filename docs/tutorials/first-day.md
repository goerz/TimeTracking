# Track your first day

In this tutorial you create a project, track two tasks, fix a mistake, add a meeting you forgot, and read the result. It takes about ten minutes on the Mac, and you end up with real data that you can delete or keep.

## Start your first entry

Open the app. The main window shows the Timer: a bar with the field "What are you working on?", and a list that is empty for now.

```{figure} /images/mac-timer.png
:alt: The Timer in the Mac app's main window, with a sidebar on the left and the running entry beside the list of recent entries.

The Timer with a running entry and a few days of history. On a new install the list is empty.
```

Type `Write the outline` into the field and press Return. The entry is running: the label next to the field counts up from `0:00:00`, the green start button has become a red stop button, and a row with the same task appears at the top of the list under "Today".

Look at the top right of your screen. The dial in the menu bar now shows the elapsed time as well.

## Give it a project

The running entry has no project yet, which the chip below the task field shows as "No project". Click the chip. A picker opens with a search field. Type `Thesis`, then click "+ Create Project “Thesis”".

A dialog appears, because every project belongs to a client. Open the Client menu and choose "New Client…". Enter `University` as the name, pick a color if you like, and confirm. Back in the dialog, click Create.

The chip now shows the project and its client. The project applies to the running entry at once; you didn't have to stop anything.

## Switch to something else

While an entry runs, the task field belongs to that entry, so typing there would rename it. To start something new, press ⌘N. The first entry stops at this instant, a new empty entry starts, and the cursor sits in the task field. Type `Email` and press Return.

Now go back to the outline. Hover over its row in the list and click the green ▶ button that appears, or double-click the row. That is "continue": a new entry starts with the same task and project, and the email entry stops. The list now holds three rows.

Press ⌘S to stop the timer.

## Fix a mistake

Say you started the outline ten minutes earlier than the app thinks. Click its first row in the list. A popover opens with the fields Task, Project, Start, Stop, Duration, and Date.

Click into Start, type a time ten minutes earlier, and press Return. The duration updates, and so does the day total in the list header. There is no Save button; every field stores its value when you press Return or leave the field. Press Escape to close the popover.

If you make a change you regret, ⌘Z undoes it.

## Add something you forgot

The Timer creates entries only by starting them. Time that is already over goes into the Calendar. Press ⌘2.

Each day has up to three columns. The wide one, Tracked, holds your entries as colored cards. Find a free stretch earlier today in that column and drag downward across it. A popover opens on the new card. Type `Standup` as the task and press Return.

Your Calendar now shows the whole day, including the time the Timer never saw. You can move a card by dragging it, or resize it by dragging its top or bottom edge. Nothing can be placed after the current time, which a colored line marks.

## See where the day went

Press ⌘3 for Reports. The tiles at the top show the logged time and the number of entries for this week, the chart shows one bar per day, and the table below breaks the time down by area, project, and task. Click the triangle next to a row to open it.

Press ⌘1 to return to the Timer.

## What you learned

An entry has a task and a project, and at most one entry runs at a time. Starting or continuing anything stops what was running. The Timer is where you start things, and the Calendar is where you repair the past.
