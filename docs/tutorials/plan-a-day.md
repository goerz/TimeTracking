# Plan a day in the Calendar

This tutorial adds the other two columns of the Calendar. You plan a block of work, track it, and compare the plan with what happened. It needs an iCloud calendar account on your Mac and takes about fifteen minutes, spread over part of your day.

## Open the Calendar and allow access

Press ⌘2. The first time the Calendar column shows, macOS asks whether the app may use your calendars. Click Allow. Without access the column shows a one-line notice, and the rest of the view still works.

The app creates a calendar named "Time Blocks" in your iCloud account. Your plans are ordinary events in that calendar, so they also appear in Calendar.app and on your phone.

```{figure} /images/mac-calendar.png
:alt: The Calendar section with days side by side.

Each day has an Activity column, a Tracked column, and a Calendar column.
```

## Plan a time block

Find a free stretch later today in today's Calendar column, and drag down across it. Unlike in the Tracked column, you can draw here in the future. An editor opens with title, project, start, end, and notes. Enter `Write the intro` as the title, pick the project Thesis from the [previous tutorial](first-day.md), and save.

The block appears as a pale card with a dashed border in the color of the project's area. It is a plan and nothing more: the app never marks it as done and never compares it with what you tracked.

## Show your other calendars

Open Settings with ⌘, and click the Calendar pane. Under "Calendars to show", check the calendars you want beside your plans, such as work or family. Their events appear in the Calendar column as pale cards. They are read-only here; a double-click opens one in Calendar.app.

## Track the block

When the time has come, hover over the time block and click its "+" button, or drag the card into the Tracked column. A provisional entry appears with the block's title as the task and its project already chosen, ending no later than now. Press Return to keep it.

Entries can't lie in the future, so for a block that has not started this only shows a short notice. For work you are about to begin, use the Timer instead.

## Let the tracker fill in the left column

The Activity column stays empty until you enable the tracker, which is optional. If you want it, follow [Set up the activity tracker](../howto/tracker.md) and work for an hour. Gray cards then appear in the column, each naming the app, session, or site that held your attention.

These cards are a record, not entries. Where you forgot to start the timer, hover over a card and click "+", and the popover offers an entry over the longest part of the card that nothing else covers. Give it a project and press Return. [Turn activity into time entries](../howto/entries-from-activity.md) has the details.

## What you learned

The three columns answer three questions about a day: where your attention went, what you tracked, and what you planned. Only the middle column counts as tracked time. The other two exist so you can compare, and fill gaps with a drag.
